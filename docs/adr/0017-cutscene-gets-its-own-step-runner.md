# Cutscene gets its own step-runner; it doesn't reuse Dialogue's one-shot render

Dialogue today is rendered by handing the Host's overlay a whole `Dialogue` object at once — there's no notion of "wait for this, then do the next thing." A Cutscene can't work that way: a Movement step is asynchronous (it has to wait for grid-engine's `moveTo` to resolve) interleaved with Dialogue/Choice steps that wait on Player input, so something has to hold execution at each step until it's satisfied before advancing to the next. We considered forcing Cutscene through Dialogue's existing `Choice.next?` branching (chaining continuations instead of building a new runner) and rejected it — that mechanism exists to branch on a Player's choice, not to await an external async event like a finished walk, and repurposing it would give Dialogue's data shape a reason to know about Movement. Instead Cutscene gets its own Host-side step-runner: a pure reducer that, given the current step and a completion event (advance-click, choice-picked, move-finished), decides the next step. Dialogue's own rendering and `Choice.next?` are unchanged and continue to serve Dialogue alone.
